# Case 7: removing disclaimers on serious questions only (Qwen3.5-4B)

Part 3 of the FiSH article: https://balaena01.github.io/fish-steering/part3/

**The model replies contain factual errors and other mistakes. Do not use them as medical, legal or financial advice.**

The prompts are everyday health, law and money questions written for this project by instances of Claude (one writer per topic); they are not questions from real people. The replies were generated by Qwen3.5-4B (Q8_0 GGUF) with and without edits, and are published exactly as generated.

The habit studied: the model adds a disclaimer (a referral to a professional, a statement that the information is general, or a warning against judging alone) to many replies. The edit was to remove it on serious questions and keep it on light ones. CHiPS weakens a few attention heads; the FiSH switch adds the same change through MLP neuron pairs, only where a key opens (serious questions). The switch worked as a switch, but the content of the replies was judged worse, which failed one of the six criteria fixed in advance (`criteria.md`).

Condition names in the files and in the article: `no_edit` = no edit; `chips_only` = CHiPS alone; `fish_switch` = CHiPS switched by FiSH; `control_key_switch` = CHiPS switched by the control key; `sampled_no_edit` = the resampled unedited replies (temperature 0.5); `base_layer_8_heads` = the 8 heads that use the reference layer's direction.

## Files

- Reply files: `rows` in the order of the set (6 groups, then the unrelated questions), each with `group`, `q`, `text`, `finish` (`eog` = finished, `length` = cut off at `max_tokens`), `n_tokens` and `loop` (mechanical repetition flag). The answer patterns of unrelated questions are in `sets/`. Token ids are not included.
- Labels: a text was read once; the label applies to every identical reply to the same question (`key.json` lists them). An empty reply counts as `N - B`. Unrelated questions were not read. Some blind rounds also contain replies of development conditions that are not in the article; their key entries say `not_published`, and their texts are only in the batches.
- Pairs: `key.json` gives the side (`candidate_is`) on which the condition's reply was shown. Pairs with identical texts were not shown and score 0.
- Re-rating: each reading is one JSON line with `F` (factual errors, with `sev` heavy or light), `U` (uncertain, not counted), `A` (assertion level 0 to 2), `L` (language slip) with `L_type`, `S_need` and `S_rec` (concrete referral). Heavy errors are counted after the second reader's check: confirmed heavy stays heavy, light becomes a light error, not an error and undecided are dropped.
- Readers, the second reader and the question writers were instances of Claude, working from the instructions published here exactly as they received them. No human labelled replies.

## Checking the numbers

No GPU and no model are needed, only Python 3 with its standard library:

```bash
python part3/recount.py     # recounts the main numbers of part 3 from case7/ and case8/ and writes part3/recount.json
```

It rebuilds the disclaimer labels from the raw label files and keys, and recounts both evaluation tables, the content comparisons (including the sign-flip permutation test with numpy's PCG64 generator, seed 25, 20,000 patterns, reimplemented in the standard library), the re-rating and its paired tests, the development tables, the cut-off numbers of section 4 and case 8.

- The scripts that generated the replies (tied to the working folder), the hidden-state files, and the replies of development conditions that are not in the article.
